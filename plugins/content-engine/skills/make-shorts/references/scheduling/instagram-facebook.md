# Instagram Reels and Facebook Reels

Checked October 2026. Menu names can shift.

## In the Instagram app (simplest)

Needs a professional account (Creator or Business). If they're on a personal account:
Settings and privacy > Account type and tools > Switch to professional account. It's free.

1. Save the Short from the Ready to Post folder to their phone (open the file, tap Share > Save Video).
2. In Instagram, tap + and choose Reel. Pick the saved video.
3. Tap Next. Skip Instagram's own edit tools: the Short already has captions and music.
4. Paste the caption from `Instagram Reels caption.txt` (or tap Copy on the Posting plan page first).
5. Choose a cover: tap Edit cover and slide to a frame where their face and the hook both show.
6. Open Advanced settings (iPhone) or More options (Android), turn on Schedule this Reel, pick the date and time, tap Done.
7. Go back and tap Schedule.

To check: Profile > the menu > Scheduled content. Instagram allows scheduling up to 75 days ahead.
A scheduled Reel can't be edited: delete it and schedule it again.

Sharing to Facebook: in step 6 they can turn on "Share to Facebook" if the accounts are linked.

## In Meta Business Suite (on a computer, schedules Instagram and Facebook together)

1. Go to business.facebook.com and choose their business.
2. Open Planner (or Content) and click Create reel.
3. Tick Instagram and Facebook under "Post to".
4. Upload the Short from the Ready to Post folder.
5. Paste the caption. Facebook and Instagram can have the same caption.
6. Choose the cover frame.
7. Under Scheduling options, choose Schedule, pick the date and time, then click Schedule.

Business Suite schedules up to about 29 days ahead. Check the Planner calendar afterwards: each
Reel shows on its day.

## Common snags

- "I don't see Schedule": the account is personal, not professional, or the app needs updating.
- "The video looks blurry once posted": in the Instagram app, Settings and privacy > Data usage and
  media quality > turn on Upload at highest quality.
- "Music is muted": the engine's music bed is made in code and is safe to use. If Instagram mutes
  something, it's usually a trending sound added in the app, not ours.
